Concrete Volume Calculator

How Much Concrete Do I Need?

Measure the pour, calculate its volume, add a reasonable allowance for waste and uneven ground, then round up to the supplier's delivery or bag size. This is a planning estimate; confirm the final quantity with your supplier.

Basic formula

For a rectangular slab or footing: length × width × thickness = cubic feet. Divide cubic feet by 27 to convert to cubic yards.

Example

A 12 ft × 10 ft slab that is 4 in thick has 40 cubic feet of concrete, or about 1.48 cubic yards. With 10% allowance, plan for about 1.63 cubic yards and round up according to the supplier's minimum order.

For bagged concrete

Divide the required volume by the yield printed on the product bag. Do not assume every 60 lb or 80 lb bag has the same yield. Use the label yield, include waste, and round up to whole bags.

Before ordering

  • Use the actual thickness, not the average thickness you hope to achieve.
  • Account for irregular edges, subgrade variation and small spills.
  • Check whether the supplier sells by cubic yard, partial load or bag.
  • Verify strength, aggregate size, access and placement requirements.

FAQ

How much waste should I add?

A small, regular form may need less allowance than an irregular excavation. Use the calculator's visible waste setting as a planning assumption and ask the supplier what they recommend.

Can this replace a concrete order quote?

No. Ready-mix yield, truck minimums, site access and local conditions can change the final order.
